The Stairs of Her Mercy

Session of 25 September 2026

The party wakes in the Fieldmaster's Suite of the Grand Lodge on a clear morning, long rested, with the river wide and bright below the window and the Summer Palace visible to the south across half a mile of grassland: a landing pad with a small, highly advanced craft sitting on it, a lighthouse with its own dock, and the great statue of Kaius I looking west into the gardens. Ten days have passed since they last took stock of their holdings, so the morning begins with the bastion turn. The storehouse sells up to a thousand gold of goods. The sacristy, run by followers of the god of mathematics who hold that numbers and information must be free and true, crafts Eric a scroll of hold person for two hundred gold. The greenhouse sends Gemma a small potted plant with a note saying they took some of Pierce's seeds: it grows three pink plums every seven days, each one a lesser restoration if eaten within a day of picking, and Gemma keeps it alive with plant growth. The rest of the money goes to the horns. The smithy at Leaf will make them, the stables will train riders to carry them, and parties of the Leaf Guard will take them to the other kingdoms; Jessica was already doing the same, and Renn Tal will be asked to put the foundries of Sharn to work. Jessica's reply comes over the sending stone in her own voice: "Copy received, but be careful. Our scribes say there is significant magical interference from a very powerful source near your location. Be advised. J.G."

Gemma casts seeming over all three before anyone knocks: Deirdre Moro, Graham Cracker, and Dario as the bodyguard Sky Rizzy. The knock is breakfast. A Lodge servant named Bimples wheels in a whole roast pig on a trolley, is tipped eighty gold, and tells "Madame" that the members' rooms are open to her party during their stay, that the meeting room can be reserved, and that her colleagues are in the ballroom if she would like to join them. Over the pig the three argue about the bell. Gemma admits she messaged Joseph Roberts from the lodge door the night before, short and sweet, and he answered. Dario wants to know what stops them teleporting into the bell tower and ringing it. They go around the question of Locke Pierce: whether Ruby Nova is his insurance or his plan, whether he and Uriel could be working together, what happens to Sharn if the bell here is destroyed. Gemma makes the case for Roberts from memory: he spared them on the train, gave them a sending stone, moved people from Newham to Leaf without asking anything but a mural, and his wife Blair helped them twice. They decide to mingle and find out where he is.

Across the upstairs corridor, a man and a woman are playing a house game called random walk, in which a mechanical egg wanders the table until it knocks down some number of pins. When the two Imperials come in, the players start talking pointedly about the tides. Gemma lets them believe Deirdre is dirty enough to gamble, and Graham loses fifty gold before winning it back on a four. The man calls himself Joey Monica. Members, he says, get the front rows on the days the Empress comes down her stairs, which happens every couple of months and last happened nine or ten months ago; the woman wonders whether she is pregnant again, and says she is over two hundred years old. Then he asks Deirdre for help with "quite a few millions of barrels of barley" stuck in a customs affair, moving out of "certain war-damaged areas". When Graham mentions the summit, both of them remember they are going to bed. Everyone in the lodge is going to bed at ten in the morning, and the party works out that they hunt at night.

In the corridor they meet Brillo Myers, a textile magnate who asks whether Graham is a Rekkenmark man, and Craig, a voting member of the membership committee who tells Deirdre he looks forward to reviewing her application. Eric rolls a natural twenty on history and recognises everyone they have met as among the richest people in Eberron. Craig is the construction magnate who built the great war memorial and cemetery, which is planned for enormous expansion, and which came up in Eric's old research into Cob Cornwell. Cob Cornwell was a member here, and his portrait hangs on the grand stair among the Fieldmasters. On the stair two men pass a purse between them without looking, a coin falls, and Eric picks it up. It was minted in the Uruk-Hoth Republic. Gemma listens to them long enough to realise that their talk of hares and crossbows is a cant for business. She remembers what the party overheard at Posay four years ago: General Thar telling Esther that the Lhazaar Principalities were helping the Uruk-Hoth. The men go through a door to the south.

Pork Broccoli is waiting out front, smoking, and lowers the ladder of his cart the moment he sees them. He has been driving this road for about a year on a license he borrowed from his cousin and is nearly done paying off. He drops them in the Plaza of the Martyrs. The stones of the plaza are carved with tens of thousands of names. The Stairs of Her Mercy rise out of it in white arcs toward a colossal statue of Uriel in her Black Dove habit, and at their foot a queue of petitioners begs sisters in green habits to let them through, past white guards who stand head and shoulders over the crowd. The plaza is a market for everything from holy water and blankets to building stone and furniture, with musicians, dancers and a quinceañera watching them. Eric is struck by how free the place feels after Korth. The rich go up the stairs, and the poor wait.

The Counting House is the kind of office Eric used to work in. Inside, General Stockton Thar, with two officers, two dark druids of his staff and a room's worth of death troopers, has cornered Director Aurel Kesk. Thar's voice is ruined. He raises a hand and the officer in front of him pulls at his own collar, and when he lowers it the man can breathe. "I don't need you to have the authority. I have the authority. I am going to deliver my report to the Emperor in person. You will take me to him." Kesk answers that he counts money, that he has not been up the stairs or to the palace in more than a year and has never seen the Emperor, and that nobody can open that door but the sisters. The druids hand Kesk a tightly rolled sheet of parchment, and the delegation leaves, though several troopers stay to watch the Director. Gemma whispers her idea to the others: kill Thar, and walk up the hill wearing his face.

Kesk greets Deirdre with "The Ministry sends nobody up this hill for two years, then sends a Star Advocate. Should I be worried or flattered?" and hands her to Sub-Auditor Corvan Bleek, who was in Eric's intake twenty years ago and was a dick then. Bleek tells them the Lodge keeps its own guest records and that records are in the east wing. Officers come up the hill all the time expecting their rank to get them up the stairs, he says, but by old tradition the military is not allowed up, and Imperial power stops at the edge of the hill. He lists the sisters by colour: white, black, the green who are healers, and the red who take care of the Emperor. While he talks, an officer shakes hands with a man in a swashbuckler's coat and says he is sorry to hear about his aunt, and the swashbuckler pays Bleek a bag of platinum, which goes into a safe in a wardrobe, for a load of sealed hazardous-materials containers. Dario follows the containers outside: they go by carriage straight to the Lodge and around the back.

Down the back corridor, past death troopers lounging along the wall, are doors marked Director, Treasury, and Records, the last with Wardress I. Vitt's nameplate and an out-to-lunch sign. In the commissary Eric recognises Ilma Vitt, a grey dwarf of sixty with ink to her elbows: she trained him, signed him into the Hall of Records, and wrote the assessment that got him the job, "a mediocre accountant with an unusual eye." Two clerics of the same god are complaining to her that the probate line has grown so long they had to shut the doors, and that they are being made accomplices to the corruption of their superiors; one also complains that the Lodge charged her full price. Vitt nods to the Ministry uniforms and leaves.

In her office she tells Deirdre she is the first person from the Ministry to come up that hill in two years, and hands over her private reconciliation of every probate the Counting House has handled, in her own cipher. The Director and his whole staff, she says, are enriching themselves on the property of people who die in the hospital, on donations of land and goods, and on straightforward war profiteering, and they should hang for it. The Lodge's books would complete the chain. "You can't trust the Director." The key is eight letters, and it is what you do when you really love someone. Eric tries MARRIAGE, and Vitt, reading over his shoulder, says "You cross your sevens. That's very unusual. Where did you learn accounting?" before telling him it is MULTIPLY. Then she walks out and locks the door behind her.

An hour of decoding confirms everything she said and shows that it is half the proof. Patients consign their bodies to the Emperor and their belongings to the church to cover their care. Gunpowder, textiles, building materials, precious metals, artillery and even ships are donated to the church and sold out the door at bargain prices. The Lodge holds the other half. Then Kesk knocks, with three troopers behind him, two of them holding Vitt by the shoulders, and demands to know why the Advocate ordered his wardress out of her own office without a warrant. Eric sees at once that she is under duress. Graham produces the hold person scroll as a warrant signed by the Magister, Deirdre sells it, and Kesk says, "I can't believe that Merkin, of all people, would pull this." His men drag Vitt into his office. When Deirdre follows, they are beating her. Deirdre demands her as a witness, Kesk lets her go, and he tells the Advocate that Freyd can forget the favour Kesk was going to do him, that there are more important people than he who will be upset if she holds too hard to the letter of the law, and that he can make cooperation very worth her while. Vitt says they have to get out now. She stays at the pilgrim camp most nights and will meet them there. On the way out, Eric gives Bleek a salute of their shared faith and says, "Be seeing you."

Outside, Thar is forcing his way through the petitioners to the foot of the stairs, and the crowd follows in his wake. Two sisters bow, welcome him, and tell him his name is on the list; the white guards step up behind them. When the crowd starts pleading its own cases, Thar says "Enough!" and a supernatural silence falls over the plaza. The sisters send for a high sister. It takes fifteen or twenty minutes for her procession to come down the stairs one step at a time: sisters in red, led by a very tall woman in black wearing a red mask. The crowd parts, and even the druids stand aside. She tells Thar the Emperor is very ill but has agreed to meet him in his garden on the morrow. "Excellent. Thank you, Sister, by his grace and her mercy." Thar speaks a few words in a language none of them know, becomes a huge red bird, and flies with his two druids, now smaller brown and grey birds, first to the Counting House roof and then north across the river.

The plaza tells them the rest. The Empress heals in person but touches only five or six people, and nobody can predict which. A woman who has waited five months insists she has seen people come down healed, though most who go up do not come back down the stairs. A man in a turban offers to broker their donation for twenty percent and says it takes a month to pray properly up there. A grey sister, a black sister and a very tall white sister whom Gemma knows from Korth walk down through the crowd, and the man flinches out of her way without seeing her. People say there are more sisters in the valley than there were a year ago, and that the Grand Lodge will not take pilgrims at any price. When Deirdre asks at the queue, the sister's book has no appointment for her.

The man in the turban takes them to his brothers' food stall on Relic Row, the twins Bran Flakes and Frosted Flakes (Frosted has grey in his hair and is the kinder of the two). Their price for a place on the list within a day is three and a half thousand gold: two and a half to them and their supplier, and a thousand to be left on the altars on the way up, in the right amounts at the right altars. They also sell water from the Fountain of Mercy at a silver a flask, medallions, habits and copies of the Litany. Asked about the floating object on the hill, Frosted shivers. It is new, he says, and sisters tend it night and day and never stop looking at it, and it feels as though it is watching you. He goes up often with a borrowed cane, washes his face in the fountain, and says it is like a whole night's sleep in one splash and fixes his back. Up there, he says, is a garden on the east side with an orrery and a seeing device that looks all the way down the river; the Tomb of Kaius I in front of the western nave; the Lazaret in the western nave itself; and beyond, stairs south to the Imperial gardens, where he has never been. The party does not trust the fountain. They float two plans for later: go back to the Lodge as big-game hunters and recruit the night hunters to shoot a huge red bird, then walk into the garden tomorrow as General Thar; or rob the tomb.

For now they buy nothing. Behind the stables, where a path runs toward the pilgrim camp's tents, Gemma recasts seeming, and the three become Black Doves: Gemma as the novice she was in Korth, Eric as a classic old nun, and Dario as a winged aasimar sister, which does nothing to disguise how he walks. Gemma rolls a natural twenty on performance, and the guards hurry them up the stairs.

The landings are altars, each built for one of the old gods and rededicated to the Mother. They pass an old halfling blessing them from the first small landing. At the first great altar they overhear Tamsin Vell, a vine-dresser whose vineyards are three miles out, who climbs to this landing every morning to pray for a son missing at the front for two years, and Lord Aldric and Lady Sabine Varenne, who have come to hunt and to watch the pilgrims; Lady Sabine complains that the candles smell of tallow, and Lord Aldric says he has just brought the family silver down for the estate appraiser at the Lodge. Old Jim Smallberries is there too, up on a pass after hauling a delivery, and goes around a corner and is gone. A sister in green watches the three of them curiously. Above a tree that was once an altar to a nature god, Uriel's statue stands with shield and imperial crown over a fountain at her feet. Dario spits into the fountain. The pilgrims gasp, Gemma turns on him, and he says he is glad he did not piss in it. A passing man tells the aasimar nun her voice is beautiful. Eric, asked how his faith is holding up, says he cares about maths, not the gods. They pass a boy of twelve dragging his grandfather up the stairs, a man with gout in both feet, an old woman carried by her nephew in a chair, an altar heaped with gold and jewels, and a victory wreath for the fallen, and come out onto the terrace before Nos Matre.

The Polygon floats in the middle of the forecourt, sharp-edged and swirling grey, pink, white and sometimes black, reflecting the sky and bending it, and four strangely dressed sisters stand one to a side staring up at it without moving. Gemma feels sick at the sight of it, and Eric feels a rush of hate through his veins. He casts detect magic and comes closer than the sisters do: enchantment, with transmutation holding it in the air, and an arcana check tells him it is a store of immense power that can cast spells. It is Uriel's focus, her version of the thing that floated outside LeBeefe's stronghold. The tall sister comes down past them and gives them a look; Gemma keeps her eyes where a novice's belong. Just inside the doors of the nave stands a great fountain of glittering pale blue water, and Gemma knows it: it is the fountain from her vision with Kitsune, which ran with blood there. They end the session at the Tomb of Kaius I, which carries abjuration magic, arguing about which Kaius ended the Last War and whether to break into it. Eric knows from LeBeefe that the Emperor may be a vampire; the others have only rumour and Master Armhair's dragon.

Experience: 2,500 each. Next: the Lazaret, or the Lodge's night hunt for a red bird before Thar's audience in the garden tomorrow; Ilma Vitt is waiting at the pilgrim camp.

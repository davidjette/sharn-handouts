The Death Warrant and the Ringer

Session of 7 August 2026

TL;DR

The trio wakes to two dreams and a missing child. Eric's nightmare puts him back in the Glitterhame, naked among indifferent gricks, hearing the whisper he knows — except this time it is not aimed at him: around a fungal growth he finds John C. LeBeefe standing over a little duergar boy, "Yes, my little one. Good idea to ring that bell, a jolly good idea." He wakes to the camp whispering that Egg Roll never came back from fetching water. Gemma dreams of a marble hall, her patron Kitsune watching from a chaise, and a starved old man resembling the Emperor walking in with an infant and blood pouring from his chest, falling into a fountain that turns instantly red. Madame Tso tells Gemma the boy is not her concern; the boy's mother, searching his bedroll, hands over a hand-drawn map of the entire foundry level, made by Egg Roll himself — better than anything the kin have. It becomes the session's most valuable item. Behind the map's secret doors the trio clears the level room by room: the sealed Black Dove shrine (area 42) — its seal signed by Sister Maelis Dorn — where Overseer Velen Marsk and two risen ogres are still on shift ("Back to work"), and where Marsk's satchel yields the session's political bomb: a writ of warrant for the death of a long list of names, beginning with Corn Deeptempura and Sedda Deeptempura, including Sister Calvane, ending with Velen Marsk himself, signed by Emperor Caius III — kill everyone, then kill yourself. Most names are crossed out; the drains in the floor are caked with three-year-old blood. Dario finds ~280 gp of dwarven grave-jewels on a body on the altar, leaves them where they lie, and tells the kin they are there — the exact inverse of last session. In Calvane's library Gemma rolls a natural 20 on Religion and pulls out the consecration rite for the Fatanya Nyel: thirteen Doves, an invocation of all thirteen planes, the bells bound to "the unholy blood and the great mother," and a very dear sacrifice — a living child or virgin royalty. They take the whole 50-pound library. Eric, meanwhile, spends the day arguing that the bell might be worth ringing ("whoever rings the bell, they control the magic"), is told flatly that Gemma will punch him in the face and later that she will kill him in his sleep, and deflects — "I haven't mentioned it all day." Finally they take the chain ladder down: Eric fails the climb, falls ~150 feet, takes 43 bludgeoning and a sprained ankle, and Dario lays on hands. At the bottom — black water, the smell of stomach acid, and drag marks "large and reptilian" — they spot a figure sorting through barrels, and it is Sedda Tiddes Deeptempura, three years dead, decayed, and lucid. She mistakes them for the Lord Mayor's extraction team; Gemma runs the con at Deception 27 and gets everything: the Speaker in the Glitterhame that knew every guard's movements, the months of whispering, the plan (ring the bell, kill the Doves and the Imperials, and we would be safe), her own confession that she rang it — with an improvised hammer, a rock — and came straight down here as arranged. "And I'm still here." Eric blurts "the man is dead"; she feels the connection between them, and the con collapses into the truth. "Oh God, I'm undead. This is horrible." She gives them the two things that matter — the horn is under a secret panel beneath the rug in her father's study, and her father's hammer can crack anything made of black Kyber — asks them to end her, and Dario gives her last rites and takes her head. A locate object ritual then delivers the session's joke at the party's expense: the horn is 300 feet straight up. They had walked past it. Back in the study, under the rug, a sliding panel, a stair, and a spell-sealed chest opened with a key they were already carrying: the lead-bound folio — bell castings, tempering methods, harmonic-frequency mathematics, a margin note reading "wounding" beside a hammer striking a bell, and on the last page, in a shaking hand, "I made their key. Someone must learn to make its answer sing." — and the coiled brass horn, Corn's smith-mark on it. The folio is a Tome of Understanding. Dario carries Sedda's remains through the camp to the freed shrine, lays her beside the unknown soldier, and he and General Tso consecrate Sedda Deeptempura, princess and heir. Madame Tso returns her late husband's Belt of Dwarvenkind to Eric and blesses the party's claim on everything else. The kin name the hammer: KNELL. They did not meet Vuldrach. Egg Roll is still missing.

Beat-by-beat

1. Two dreams and a missing child [0:10–0:29]

Eric's fitful sleep: the Glitterhame again, soft mossy rock underfoot, nude and barefoot, gricks and worse crawling past and paying him no heed. The whisper is there — but not in his ear this time. It is over there, being spoken to someone else. He moves closer, looks around a large fungal growth, and finds a little duergar boy being looked down at by John C. LeBeefe, equally nude, looking like he's about to eat him whole. LeBeefe looks up at Eric. He wakes.

The camp is awake and whispering. General Tso comes out; a woman says "It's Egg Roll." Perception 28 (his first of two natural 20s in ten minutes): the boy went for water three hours ago and never came back. A kin argues "let's tell the pink skins, maybe they'll go look for him" and the General shuts it down — "Don't talk to them. Just go look for him." Hetta steps out of the shadows near Eric, whispers with the General, and is sent off.

Gemma dreams of a beautiful marble foyer, a grand fountain, gold and murals, and Kitsune sitting on a chaise in a white toga — regarding her, saying nothing, then looking pointedly at the far wall. The doors burst open and out waddles an old man with a scraggly beard who looks like he hasn't eaten in years, blood pouring from his chest, holding an infant. He falls into the fountain; the water goes red instantly, spills across the white floor, reaches her feet, and she wakes. Later confirmed: he resembles the Emperor. She never sees the child's sex (Perception 14).

Dario dreams about band practice, unprepared, and takes some abuse for it.

2. "It's not your concern" [0:26–0:48]

Eric tells Gemma. Gemma knocks on Madame Tso's door and gets the door shut in her face — "It's not your concern." "Mind your own business." Pressed on whether the boy could have gone down: "There's no way he went down. Because it's very dangerous below." But: if you see a small duergar child, tell him to come home right away.

Gemma finds the mother instead, crying. The exchange is prickly — "What do you expect? Trying to get some kind of reward? Trying to blackmail me if you find my son?" — and Gemma answers it straight: "No. I want to get the fuck out of here as fast as possible. But while I'm doing what I'm doing here, if I see your son, I will get him back to you." Green tunic. Short. He was poking around the foundry for gold.

Then the mother reaches into her bag.

A hand-drawn map of the whole level. Beautifully done. Egg Roll drew it.

"He didn't mark it at all, but he obviously found a lot more than the rest of us did."

Eric's second natural 20 reads it: the mapping stops at area 35, the statue room — the last thing the boy drew was the way the party themselves came in. "This kid's smart. Good kid. I hope he lives."

3. Kyber, and a sword that changed [0:53–1:03]

Over the map, Dario delivers the session's lore set-piece. Kyber is not a term of art but a common word: one of the three progenitor dragons, encased in the body of Eberron, and the ore is called Kyber from old myths that it is its body, blood, or outer scales. Black Kyber is Shavat. Red Kyber is something else entirely — "something we've seen used to horrible ends." Shown Bright Dawn, which used to be red Kyber and is now crystalline, he turns it over twice: "I remember when you drew this sword. I thought it might be cursed — that it might curse you the way Crona's sword cursed her. But this… no. It couldn't. Could it?" Then the myth of rocks falling from the sky — the shed scales of Siberys, "the shards of the moon," white Kyber, which he corrects to moonstone, because "people are imprecise when talking about metallurgy."

And his working theory of the foundry: the dwarves were transmuting black Kyber into a more complex crystalline form that could take imprinted arcane sigils dictated by the elves who enslaved them. He points at the bell's mark — the master's insignia stamped upside down. An old smith's curse. It means they were under duress when they made it.

4. The sealed shrine, and the Overseer [1:03–1:50]

The map shows a shroud and a body in area 42. They find a consecrated door: a white ceremonial sheet, modest geometric embroidery, meticulously sewn into the wood frame as a sealing ritual, with a writ of binding from Sister Maelis Dorn — the Black Dove Gemma befriended undercover in Korth, who confronted the Lissander at the Peace Summit. The duergar have left it alone. The working theory holds: she came here after the bell rang, sealed this, and probably performed the ritual on the bell itself.

Dario reaches up and rips the cloth down.

Inside the ruined shrine: a big man's corpse in a breastplate, rod in hand, satchel at his side, face mostly gone but the eyes still there — and two enormous undead beside him, "large dudes" who have clearly grown larger in their undead lives. He points the rod immediately.

"You — get back to work."

"Do you know who I am? I am the Overseer. Overseer Velen Marsk. And I have given you an order. Complete it. Kill every dwarf in this foundry, or you will join them in the lake."

"I don't know who it was that locked this door. But as soon as I find them, their head will go into the forge."

Dario steps up — "I don't work for you" — draws, lights the blade, "I work with flame" — and opens with Abjure Foes, frightening Marsk and one brute. Gemma opens the ogre's face and dreadful-steps into a humongous pile of bones — dozens, maybe hundreds of people dumped at the altar. Marsk's Foreman's Voice keeps calling the room to order ("On the double. We need to make the count.") and raises the bones in waves; Eric eventually jumps on a pew, presents his holy symbol and turns the lot of them — Marsk saves, everything else fails and flees. They gang up and put him down.

5. The death warrant [2:15–2:35]

Marsk's satchel:

  • The Korranberg Foundry personnel ledger and administration book, his name on the cover, many spent quills.
  • A bone tube with silver caps — spell scrolls of web and spider climb. It becomes Gemma's scroll case.
  • A scroll on high-quality parchment, black ribbon, seal of the holy see of the Black Doves, hastily retied.

The scroll is an Imperial decree.

A writ of warrant for the death of the following — and then a tremendous list of handwritten names.

It begins Corn Deeptempura. Sedda Tiddes Deeptempura. It goes down from there.

Sister Calvane is on it.

The last name on the list is Velen Marsk — and Marsk signed to receive it.

Most of the names are crossed out. Marsk's is not. Calvane's is not. Some dwarves are noted as missing or potentially escaped.

It is signed Caius III.

Kill everyone, then kill yourself. And this room is where it was processed: administrative papers still on the table, names crossed off as they were dealt with, and the large floor drains — the kind used to clean a big space — caked with blackened, years-old blood.

Eric: "This is proof of their crimes. We have to preserve this. This could be important in the trials that follow."

Gemma keeps circling the thing that doesn't fit: "Why bring them down there? Why not just kill them?" — which is the first time anyone at the table says out loud that the Empire may have had a deal with the dragon.

6. What Dario leaves behind [2:29–2:33]

On the altar, a small-statured skeleton, not risen, wearing about 280 gp of dwarven jewels — taken out of a tomb.

Dario leaves them exactly where they are, walks to the door, and tells the kin the room is cleansed and that there are valuables on one of their fallen. He is snapped at anyway — "You leave it alone. That belongs to Deeptempura as part of our inheritance" — and answers, "I know. That's why I'm telling you about it."

The kid on the giant spider, Wonton, gets his ears boxed by his own people for it: "Shut your hole, Wonton. You were so scared you wouldn't even go near this door. These people came in here and cleared it out in five minutes."

7. Door by door [2:35–3:40]

The rest of the level, methodically, off Egg Roll's map: a secret door on the south wall of the shrine opening onto the corridor where they first met Wonton and the spider; storerooms of dry broken barrels and mushrooms; an empty armory; zombies; a room of goblins. Nothing that changes the story, and a great deal of it — the kin have been managing less than a room a day and the trio does the level in an afternoon.

8. The library [3:42–3:47]

Back in Calvane's suite, the shelves nobody had read. Black Dove tomes: histories, religious protocols, rites and rituals. Gemma rolls a natural 20 on Religion with guidance and finds, in a protected but central place on the shelf:

The consecration rite for the Fatanya Nyel.

The full ritual, performed by a group of thirteen Doves. Afterward the bells are connected to "the unholy blood and the great mother." It involves an invocation of magic from all thirteen known planes of Eberron.

And a very dear sacrifice — "usually a living child, or virgin royalty."

It is profoundly unholy, and it makes the Black Doves' light-cleric facade "a real sham." They take the lot: ~50 pounds of library into Dario's bag, ~400 gp of religious icons, crystals and foci, and Sister Calvane's holy symbol (25 gp). The demon's body is gone — she dripped through the floor when she died.

9. "I haven't mentioned it all day" [4:15–4:29]

The argument the party has been not-having since the Bladeworks. Eric keeps steering back toward the bell — let's go look at it one more time; there might be a spell written on it; there are lots of different kinds of bells, maybe some you can ring — and when pushed on why he wanted to ring it the night before, gives the tell straight out:

"Well, whoever rings the bell, they control the magic, right?" — "Controlling an undead army might not be a bad thing."

Gemma: "No. It turns people crazy and rises the dead." And then, plainly: "The only way to find out, Eric, is for me to punch you in the face if you try to, because I will."

Eric's answer is the most alarming thing he says all session: "All I know is you're the one obsessed with the bell. I didn't even mention the bell. I haven't mentioned it all day."

They go down instead. Dario, asked what he knows: true dragons are rare and secretive, black dragons breathe acid, lair near water, and hoard jealously — and "they are my progenitors as much as they are yours, but I descend from the couatls."

10. Down the ladder [4:35–4:49]

Past a secret door and a deep pit: a chain-and-metal ladder descending far into the darkness. Roughly 300 feet, air getting wetter the whole way, "the smell of rot and fumes, almost like stomach acid — you're descending into the bile and the bowels of some great creature."

Athletics to descend. Gemma 12, fine. Eric rolls a 3 and falls.

"Eric, you fall into space."

No feather fall, no reaction. 43 bludgeoning damage, flat on his back, prone, all the air out of him, and a sprained ankle halving his speed. He hit stone rather than water — the DM notes water would have hurt less. Dario climbs down without falling and lays on hands for 25, ankle included.

At the bottom, near-verbatim from the module: the chasm stream landing in white noise and spray, into a pool that churns and settles into black; water that smells like stomach acid; a shingle bank running north. Drag marks. Gemma's Survival 21: "large and reptilian." A ruined-but-crossable dwarven bridge north; the water splitting, some west, some east under the bridge.

And to the west, on a Perception 14: a shuffling figure, checking boxes, looking in barrels, moving things back and forth. "Do zombies normally take orders?" "Zombies don't usually bother organizing their things."

11. Sedda [5:01–5:14]

Dario sneaks along a crack barely wide enough to walk through; his armor clinks; the thing looks up. A dwarvish woman, long decayed, but somehow lucid — both eyes and most of the jaw still there.

"Are you here? Are you finally here?"

"It did not work as the Lord Mayor described. It was horrible. Much worse than he said."

"Are you here to help me recover what we can? The Lord Mayor needs you to confirm to me that I'm talking to the right person."

"I'm Sedda Tiddes."

Gemma takes the con — of course we are here for you — and burns a Lucky on a Deception 27. Everything comes out:

  • Her work duty was changed: carting waste down into the Glitterhame. Punishment — they had caught her "breaking protocol with my meetings" and stealing provisions and equipment.
  • The Glitterhame had been off limits since the fungal bloom started. Down there she heard a voice — "a speaker" — that gave her tremendous ideas and seemed to know the movements of every Imperial guard and administrator.
  • She told it about her father's horn, and how he thought it might work. "It encouraged me." It called itself the Mayor. It spoke to her for months; after a while she could hear it all the time.
  • The plan: "for me to ring the bell — that this would kill the Doves and all the Imperials, that we would be safe."
  • "Who rang the bell?" — "I did. I rang it and came straight down here like the plan was. And I'm still here."
  • She expects to be given the foundry when the Lord Mayor takes the throne.

Eric says it out loud: "The man is dead." Gemma patches over it — he is only dead in that we have not completed our quest; there is an outsider, a witch, a witch of death, trying to overthrow the mayor — but Sedda has already looked at Eric and felt the connection between them.

"Something's not right. I'm dead."

"It didn't work, did it?"

She rang it with an improvised hammer — a rock, not the proper one. Something happened, but not what was promised.

"Oh God, I'm undead. This is horrible. My people — they've been destroyed."

"You have to stop this from happening everywhere."

And then she gives them the session:

"My father's horn. It is in his study. There's a secret panel below the rug in the center of the room."

"If you play the fanfare of dawn on a horn built the way my father designed — any bard with enough talent can drown out the sound of the bells."

"But the bell cannot be unrung, can it?" — "My father's hammer can crack anything made of black Kyber. If you find that, you could also destroy the bells."

"But I must ask, if you favor — end this suffering. I am less than myself."

She falls to her knees. Gemma promises her remains will go to her ancestors and be buried properly. Dario draws:

"I commit you to the hands of your maker. And I absolve you of all the sins for which you are truly repentant. May the flame have mercy on us all."

Body and head both go into the bag. Her hideaway turns out to be the staging ground of her rebellion — months or years of purloined supplies, now old trash.

12. Three hundred feet straight up [4:54–5:26]

Eric had ritual-cast locate object before the conversation, looking for a Deeptempura brass horn. The answer lands while they're standing at the bottom of the world:

"You sense strongly that a horn is above you. About three hundred feet."

"So the horn is not down here at all. We must have overlooked it."

Dario carries Eric back up on his wings. In the study they toss the rug — "motherfucking rug" — and find sliding panels, a stair down, and a chest guarded by a statue of a dwarven elder and sealed by a spell. No mechanical lock; the key Gemma had already found in this room slides in and the box opens. Nobody has opened it since the massacre.

Inside:

  • The lead-bound folio, in the clan shorthand — drawings of bells and of the casting of the first bell; tempering and hammering methods; mathematical figures Eric recognizes at once as harmonic frequencies and Fourier transforms. Margin notes crowded with question marks, one word legible — "wounding" — beside a picture of a big hammer striking a bell. Drawings of horns, worked against harmonic-resonance math, "trying to solve some type of problem related to the common symmetries of multiple waveforms."
  • On the last page, in plain Common, in a shaking hand: "I made their key. Someone must learn to make its answer sing." Beneath it, a simple set of notes — the fugue, expanded in the sheet music they already took from this room.
  • The horn. Wrapped in oilcloth. Coiled brass, unornamented, a couple of mouthpieces, an unusual asymmetrical shape, smith-marked with Corn's mark. Brass — not Kyber.
  • And the folio is a Tome of Understanding (+Wisdom, max 30; 48 hours' study over 60 days), a treatise on metallurgy and leadership that leans on the metaphor of mixing alloys as the ingredients of a strong civilization.

13. Consecration [5:27–5:31]

Dario picks up the remains and carries them through the middle of the camp. The clan comes out to see what is happening. He takes a right, heads for the shrine they cleared that morning, and lays her on the altar beside the unknown soldier. He begins a Silver Flame prayer in Dwarvish; General Tso comes over and continues the rite.

They consecrate Sedda Tiddes Deeptempura — princess, and heir.

Madame Tso presents Eric with her late husband's Belt of Dwarvenkind. The kin bless the party's possession of everything else they carried out, folio and horn included, and Bofric will translate the shorthand — a long rest's work.

Asked about the hammer in the drawing, the kin name it: KNELL — the royal hammer, King Corn's personal black-Kyber warhammer, older than the bells, and the thing they believe can break them.

Session ends on the plan: find Knell, which means finding King Corn, which means the lake and the dragon.

Intel & open threads

  • The death warrant is signed by Emperor Caius III, names Corn and Sedda Deeptempura at the top, includes Sister Calvane, and ends with Velen Marsk himself — kill them all, then kill yourself. Marsk's name is uncrossed; so is Calvane's; several dwarves are marked missing or escaped. Eric is carrying it as evidence "for the trials that follow." This is the single most politically explosive object the party has ever held.
  • The consecration rite, in full: thirteen Doves, all thirteen planes, the bells bound to "the unholy blood and the great mother" — and a living child or virgin royalty as the sacrifice. Set beside Gemma's dream (the Emperor, bleeding, carrying an infant) and the party's standing read of Uriel's thirteenth child, this is no longer a theory.
  • The horn works like this: play the fanfare of dawn on a horn built to Corn's design and any sufficiently talented bard can drown out the bells. The party immediately noted Eric's charisma is wrong for it — and that Richard Blaze is the most famous bard alive and is believed dead.
  • KNELL — King Corn's black-Kyber warhammer, older than the bells, can crack anything made of black Kyber. Presumed to be with his remains; presumed to be at the lake, with the dragon. This is now the party's stated next objective, and their route to breaking the Thirteenth here and the two they know of in Sharn.
  • Sedda's testimony closes the loop on the bloom. The Speaker in the Glitterhame knew every guard's movement, courted her for months, learned about her father's horn from her, and converted a slave rebellion into a bell-ringing. She rang it with an improvised hammer and came straight down as instructed. The party's own synthesis afterward: she was infected by the burst terrarium from LeBeefe's courier, and anyone infected who dies comes back like her — lucid, and able to be reasoned with — as against the Black Dove undead, who cannot.
  • Eric is worse, and hiding it worse. He argued for ringing the bell in the open ("whoever rings the bell, they control the magic", "controlling an undead army might not be a bad thing"), then denied having raised it at all. Gemma has now told him she will punch him, and told the table she will kill him in his sleep if he "fucks around." He still has not told them the whispers are ongoing — and Sedda felt the connection to him in front of everyone.
  • Egg Roll is still missing, and nobody has looked for him but Hetta. The party has his map, his mother's description (green tunic, short), and Eric's dream of LeBeefe standing over a duergar boy. Madame Tso insists there is no way down; the mother says six of them took a rope down and all six died.
  • Sister Maelis Dorn sealed the shrine door after the bell rang and is presumed to have performed the ritual on the bell itself. Her whereabouts are unknown and she is a friend of Gemma's.
  • The kin's position on the pact: they are willing to accept the same bargain with the same fiend that King Corn made, to retake the hold. Nobody has told them what that costs.
  • Standing: fully repaired. Grave-goods left untouched and reported, the level cleared, the princess brought home and consecrated, the Belt of Dwarvenkind returned, and the folio and horn blessed as theirs. The five jeweled belts from the sepulchers are the only unresolved theft.
  • Loot: Egg Roll's map; the Emperor's death warrant; the Korranberg personnel ledger; scrolls of web and spider climb plus a bone scroll-case; ~50 lb of Black Dove library including the consecration rite; ~400 gp of religious icons and foci; Sister Calvane's holy symbol (25 gp); the folio (Tome of Understanding); the horn; the Belt of Dwarvenkind returned. Left deliberately: ~280 gp of grave-jewels on the altar body.
  • They never met Vuldrach. "We did not meet Voldrak. That's okay — we did kill like a million fucking skeletons."
  • XP not tallied at the table — the DM will add it up. Party is level 11.

FIELD DEBRIEF — K-KORTH

Source: FOXTALE   Reliability: A (established, cover intact)
Handling: Read once, forward nothing, name no names aloud. FOXTALE remains in play — every copy of this page is a knife at her back.
Subject: The Black Dove church, Korth — the silent bell, the Litany, and the Sister-Inquisitor.

1.Entry was as a lay penitent of the Host seeking work in the mercy-kitchens. The sisters do not vet the grieving — grief is their coin, and I had rehearsed mine. Eleven days inside the Korth house. What follows is eyes-on or first-voice; I flag anything weaker.

2.The bell is real and it is the heart of the house. Cathedral-grade casting hung in the crossing of the old Host cathedral, dark metal, no rope, no clapper wheel — it is not rigged to be rung by anyone below. The sisters keep a washing-liturgy at dawn: three novices, white cloths, total silence. I carried water for it twice. The metal is warm. Dawn in Korth in winter, and the metal is warm.

3.Sung services replace all ringing. Standing under the crossing during the night office I could hear — not hear; count — a slow pressure, like a pulse behind the ears, roughly one beat to every four of a heart. The novices call it the Mother's breathing and are proud of it. I did not sleep well inside, and I do not think I was meant to.

4.The Litany. Recited at the veiling of a novice, under seal of initiation. I took it from Sister Maelis Dorn's own mouth at a veiling on my ninth day — she led the rite, and I stood in the choir of witnesses with my face working correctly. Verbatim:

come the night / the bells will toll / thirteen / the dead will rise / to serve the living / and the dragon / will be reborn

 The novices treat it as poetry. Dorn does not. She counts the "thirteen" on her fingers as she says it — an accounting habit, not a flourish. To her it is a checklist.

5.Sister-Inquisitor Maelis Dorn. Aereni, young-faced, spine like a drawn bow. The Korth house defers to her absolutely and she belongs to no one in it. She preaches mercy in the square and the crowds love her; inside the walls she inspects the bell the way an armorer inspects a blade before a duel. She is the only person I saw touch it bare-handed. The sisters say — first-voice, a talkative novice — that Dorn stood at the Empress's own veiling. She is Uriel's will when Uriel does not choose to appear. You saw the proof of that at Thronehold; I saw the domestic version, and it is worse.

6.The Chorus Ledger. A bound registry that arrives and departs with Dorn — grey leather, dove seal in white wax, chained to a courier-case that never leaves her reach or her cell. A novice called it the mother-book of the Houses: which house, which bell, which consecration-day. I had one look at it closed and none at it open. If a single document maps the network, it is this one, and it travels under that seal.

7.Movement pattern. Dorn keeps a circuit: Korth, then east and south by private coach under escort, gone between nineteen and twenty-six days, then Korth again. The sisters say she goes to the Mother's house to report. Cross-walked against gate logs redacted redacted and a muleteer's account in the Fulcrum material — their Scrap 14 is her escort, I am certain of it — the circuit's far end is the hospital in the mountains northeast of Qarth. The dove seal goes up that road and comes back with her. Whatever the mother-book is written for lives at the top of it.

8.Assessment. Korth is a Master — if the machine-man's classification holds, it is the proof-case: cathedral casting, never rung, tended like a relic, commanded by a seal that answers to the Empress's own hand. Test every candidate in the farmers' file against Korth and you will not be fooled twice.

9.Request for tasking. I can hold the penitent cover and I can get closer to Dorn — she has begun to notice my grief approvingly, which is the door. Authorize the approach and I will travel in her shadow to the mountains myself. Decline it, and someone still has to follow that seal, because I am telling you as the only friendly eyes that have stood under one of these things: the bell is not waiting patiently. The bell is rehearsing.

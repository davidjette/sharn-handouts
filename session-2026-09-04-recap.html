S H A R N  —  E P I S O D E   R E C A P

The Condemned

Session of 4 September 2026

The Inevitables teleport from Sharn into Deirdre Moro's apartment in the centre of Korth at eight in the evening, wearing stolen faces. Gemma appears as Deirdre Moro, a Star Advocate of the Ministry of Law; Eric as Deirdre's paralegal, Graham Cracker; Dario as a registered bodyguard. Gemma killed both originals weeks earlier in this room and disposed of the bodies. The cover is long-prepared: she spent time inside the Black Dove cloister where a bell hangs, and months tracking Deirdre specifically to take her identity and use its clearance to reach the Ivory Lazaret. Korth is a walled, steam-driven capital, old and densely built, its plumbing and heating and lights all run off pressurised steam and a hydraulic fluid called aqua vitae. Columns of soldiers, living and undead, move between the barracks in the west and the rest of the city through the night. The Empire's last airships and its death troopers ring the walls from outside.

A folder has been pushed under the door before they arrive. It carries no date, only a note on Imperial letterhead from the desk of Vice Admiral Freyd Merkin reading "Where are you?" Inside is a brief against a condemned whose name and identifying details are redacted throughout: an Imperial medical records keeper charged with falsifying Imperial documents, espionage, sabotage and embezzlement, to be prosecuted at first light. In Karrnathi law the accused is called the condemned because guilt is presumed. The supporting evidence covers inflated casualty, supply and staffing reports from the Anduin theatre, a written assessment that the Battle of Anduin would be lost for want of supplies and that surrender was acceptable, personal letters mocking Imperial duty and hierarchy, and a documented love of orcish music and dance. Every letter is addressed to a beloved sister. Preliminary material that Graham had carried to the apartment before he died adds two things: a ministry-wide directive opening mass prosecutions of everyone responsible for reporting casualties, with executions expected over the following weeks, and a pending assignment sending Deirdre to the Summer Palace to seize the records of the Grand Lodge beside the Ivory Lazaret.

The party decides to throw the case rather than win it, and spends the night looking for a way to lose without breaking cover. Dario objects that losing may cost them their access to the Summer Palace and that they are not in Korth to free one saboteur. They cross the Great Plaza to the Ministry of Records in the north, are stopped at a checkpoint by a Force Captain who cites the Imperial Blackguards and Mercenary Act over Dario's unlicensed weapons, and are waved through once Gemma produces Ministry authority. Inside, the legal section has been emptied and refilled with filler and is down to a handful of volumes. What survives establishes the shape of Karrnathi justice. The system descends from the Galifar courts and was genuinely reformist when it split off, creating a judicial bureaucracy notionally independent of the throne, and its panels are mythologised as paragons who have exonerated people later celebrated as heroes. In practice it does not seek truth but atonement: the condemned may be innocent of the charge but is guilty of something, and absolution requires submission to the will of the court, which stands in for the Emperor. There is no defence counsel, no discovery, and no complex evidentiary rules, only a panel of judges whose stated function is to interrogate the prosecution. Acquittal is common. In place of a defence there is a kin and patron system, in which a person of standing qualified by the state, a spouse, a child, a sibling, an employer or a patron, may testify to the condemned's value to society. Sabotage, espionage and embezzlement all carry death; falsifying records carries a term equivalent to twenty-five human years, scaled by race and expected lifespan. Monster races receive harsher penalties, are denied advocates entirely, and have execution methods defined specifically so that they can be executed at all.

In the Hall of Records they overhear scholars discussing a black dragon sighted over the Korramont and approach one of them, an elderly half-elf loremaster named Master Armhair who advises the court on dragon lore. Gemma admits she has stood in front of the dragon and spoken with it. Armhair tells them no black dragon has been sighted in over four hundred and thirty years by written record; that all records naming specific dragons have been removed from the Hall by order; that three black dragons descended from the First, all presumed destroyed, all driven underground when Khyber formed the Manifest Zones and trapped as the caverns closed; and that Valtrex, as the youngest and smallest, was the only one able to move through the narrow ways. He also notes that dragon blood is an alchemical reagent of extraordinary potency. They arrange to meet him at midnight in the lower plaza. On the third level of the same building they shake down a clerk who forges identity papers and weapon registrations, and have Dario documented under the name Sky Rizzy, logging every weapon he carries except Knell.

At midnight Armhair arrives with his companion, a Leonin scholar named Jelton Ahn, and delivers his life's work. A great dragon called Viagra, the most powerful known and said to have toyed with the forces that govern reality, vanished from the record long before the elves came to Khorvaire, and Armhair has traced evidence of it well past that disappearance. He believes it came to King Jarret Ilwenarn of Karrnath with a bargain the king accepted; that the sick child of the Queen's Lullaby, Prince Kaius, died and was replaced; and that Kaius III is that dragon and is the same being as Kaius I. His method is contemporaneous accounts and courtiers' journals since scrubbed from the archives. He is candid that the Emperor's reported illness does not fit his hypothesis. He names the claim a blasphemy of the highest order and invites them to have him executed for it. His original manuscript was confiscated because his work led to questions the Empire did not want asked, but he hid a copy in the Hall of Records under a false index as a treatise on the mating habits of creatures of the Underdark, and he gives them the index number so that it can be preserved.

Armhair intends to travel to the Korramont to find the dragon and finish his book, and expects to die doing it. The party gives him the Crown of Seven Stars to carry down as a gift to Valtrex from the three who freed him, telling him to bargain rather than fight, warning him about the fungal infestation, and describing the route to the lake. They do not disclose that the crown is cursed beyond instructing him never to wear it; laying hands on it reveals the holder's location to its infernal creator, wearing it permits scrying, and attunement summons the devil to offer a pact that cannot be refused without becoming its target. Jelton Ahn identifies it on sight as ancient work in a rare infernal ore and says he believes it belonged to his own people. Refusing to take a treasure of that value for nothing, he hands over a small gold case bound with a glass ring containing a scroll of true resurrection. He is separately arranging forged passports to move his wife out of the city to Kabara, which the party detects and does not challenge. Retrieving Armhair's manuscript afterward, they find a family tree of Eberron's dragons traced to the progenitors, an identification of Valtrex's father, and an extended thread connecting the Mark of Death, the birth of Erandis Vol and this same dragon, confirming Renn Tal's earlier suspicion about Vol, the Eternal Guardian and Kaius. Setting that against the Litany's promise that the dragon will be reborn, the party concludes that Uriel is attempting to bear a son by a dragon, and that Valtrex, bled for three years, does not know Viagra exists and believes the heirs are his.

At dawn two stormtroopers arrive to report that Deirdre has been recorded absent from her duties and is required at the Ministry of Law immediately. Merkin intercepts her outside the courtroom, threatens court-martial for dereliction, and says he assigned her the case as a chance to demonstrate absolute allegiance to the Empire. Gemma enters the High Court, one level up and overlooked by a balcony holding an Iron Maiden, a device that kills the condemned while preserving the body intact for its second service. Eight judges are seated and proceedings have already begun. The condemned turns to face her and is unmistakably Deirdre Moro's sister. Her name is Bantide Moro.

Gemma reads the charges without expression: falsifying Imperial records, false claims of defeat of the Imperial army at Anduin, suspected sabotage and false orders to surrender, embezzlement of medical supplies and Imperial wartime resources, conspiracy to defraud official records, perjury, adultery, idolatry, high treason and sodomy. Several judges register admiration at the clarity of the delivery. Bantide, watching her sister recite her death, throws herself on the mercy of the court and states that regardless of the truth of the accusations she is guilty of having failed the Emperor. Gemma then abandons the role of advocate and testifies as kin, stating that she cannot reconcile the charges with the woman she grew up with, that her first assumption on hearing of the case was that her sister had been framed, and that she does not understand where the evidence originated. Merkin enters and objects that Advocate Moro has confused her role and is instead bearing witness for her kin, which he concedes is her right. The panel deliberates and finds the documentation insufficient to sustain the capital claims, grants the state one additional month to produce evidence, and remands the condemned to the custody of the local garrison. Bantide manages to thank her and apologise as she is removed.

Afterward Merkin tells her she has spent her only chip, that Bantide will never leave that cell or recover her post, and that she is too credulous and does not understand the contravening forces at work. He nonetheless hands her the Grand Lodge assignment in person: travel to the Summer Palace, the Ivory Lazaret and the surrounding grounds, seize all records of guests, names and dates from the Grand Lodge, and arrest any saboteurs or suspected pirates found there. The Grand Lodge is a former guild hall used as a gathering place by non-Imperial dignitaries who need to influence the Imperial seat without serving it directly. The Empire suspects dealings there with Lhazaar pirates, and the file contains a likeness of Captain Joseph L. Roberts, the Dread Pirate, Blair's husband and Gemma's former spouse. The deadline is Monday and it is Friday morning; failure to return carries a bounty. The party takes the assignment as their sanctioned route to striking distance of the Empress and concludes that Uriel is behind whatever is happening at the Lodge. The boat down the river leaves that night.

Gemma visits the jail in the northeast corner of the Ministry to determine whether Bantide is a rebel. She finds her starved, dehydrated, chained to a wall on the upper level under Imperial Red Guard, and entirely unconvinced. Bantide has been told that Deirdre assembled the evidence against her, believes it, and reads the courtroom rescue as staged to win her trust and extract information. She identifies the flaw in the performance precisely, telling her sister that the real Deirdre has to win above everything and would never have failed unless something else were happening. She refuses food, refuses to owe anything, and tells her not to speak for her again. A thieves' cant signal, learned in rebel service and used working the trains with Izaak, registers with her and is dismissed. What breaks the impasse is Eric, who tells her plainly that he agrees her sister betrayed her, that the woman in front of her is not Deirdre because he helped kill Deirdre himself, that he is Eric, Chancellor of Sharn, and that the Emperor is not a man but a dragon. He tests the Red Guard by saying so aloud in front of them and gets no reaction. Bantide asks for one thing, a holy symbol of the Silver Flame. Dario surrenders his personal prayer book, Eric passes it through the bars, and Gemma tells her outright that she is not her sister, watching her expression move from vindication to grief.

Outside, Eric receives a sending: go to the corpse chute, I am not dead, save me, one hour. Bantide has cast feign death. They locate a stair behind the prison down to the yard where bodies are collected, track the holy symbol through the building with locate object, and watch a bagged corpse drop from the chute and get stacked with the others the Empire reuses. Medicine and Arcana checks confirm the body is hers and apparently dead. An officer stops them on the way out to ask whether the prisoner was given any foreign object, poison, contaminant or cursed item; Gemma redirects him into an argument about her ruined case and the prison's failure to feed its inmates, and he ends by apologising and blaming the rations. Dispel magic ends the feign death. Eric casts invisibility on her, Dario carries her, and they return to Deirdre's apartment.

Bantide has worked for Izaak for twenty years. Her official post is Imperial medical records keeper, but she was head nurse of a Silver Flame civilian hospital and the rebellion's surgeon, supplying medical support on the sly from surgery to revivification, and she performed the operation that fitted Izaak's prosthetic leg. She uses the codename the Nightingale. She rebelled against her family and left to join an anti-war religion, which is the origin of the letters entered against her. She reports that Fulcrum has not merely lost Zilspar but has been dismantled across the continent, as though the Empire obtained a list of every name, with people pulled from every posting on invented charges and executions running for days, and that all of it began after Izaak disappeared. She refuses to believe he informed on them and believes he is still alive. She learns from the party that Zero-One was taken outside Sharn by Eden units and weeps. Too exhausted to travel and too wanted to be seen, she gives them one piece of intelligence before the session ends: an Imperial listening post on the east side of the city beside the Hall of Submission, holding the records that would explain what happened to Izaak and Zero-One.

The party ends the day 4,000 experience each the richer, still holding Knell, the horn and the folio, no longer holding the Crown of Seven Stars, and hiding a woman officially recorded as a suicide in custody. Gemma has taken a third level of fighter as a Battlemaster with Riposte, Parry and Trip Attack, and has added proficiency in performance and in the horn as an instrument, giving her advantage on performance while impersonating and making her the second person in the party who can play the fanfare of the dawn. Outstanding in the city are the listening post, the Emperor's residence in the palace, the barracks, three buildings of unknown purpose including one explicitly forbidden in the far east, the intention to teach the fanfare and the horn's construction to someone who will remain behind, and a Master bell, great casting the ninth, hanging in a Black Dove tower attached to the Imperial Palace itself. The boat to the Summer Palace leaves at nightfall and the state has one month to reassemble its case against a condemned it believes is dead.

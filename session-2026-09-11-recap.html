S H A R N  —  E P I S O D E   R E C A P

The Listening Post

Session of 11 September 2026

Bantide Moro wakes in her dead sister's apartment with four levels of exhaustion, one hit point and no spell slots, having spent a Divine Intervention to get a few back. The glee she felt on hearing that the woman in front of her had murdered Deirdre has worn off overnight and left her frightened. She will not travel. She tells them the Empire thinks she is dead and that the apartment is the safest place she has as long as Deirdre's cover holds, and that the moment it does not she needs to be somewhere else. She has never met Zero-One and knows only Zero of Sharn. She points out, mildly, that she is not a spy: she steals medical supplies, gets them to civilians, reports troop movements and stitches people up, and she is not used to people trying to kill her except with artillery. Gemma gives her the name Foxtale and not her own. Eric she already knows, and trusts, once the beard is explained as a dwarven belt. Asked whether she is any good with a tune, Bantide says she played clarinet in high school, so Gemma writes out the fanfare of dawn for her and leaves it behind with instructions to pass it to anyone else like them. Nobody knows whether the notes work sung rather than played; they have only ever used the horn. Before she sleeps she gives them the one thing she has: the listening post is the guild house across the street from Lexicon Station, beside the Hall of Submission, and it holds the records that explain what happened to Izaak.

They have six hours in Korth before the boat. They go as Imperials rather than in disguise, on the reasoning that Deirdre has a live assignment and is entitled to ask an intelligence station about Joseph Roberts. On the way out a neighbour stops them about Deirdre's cat, which has been fouling the stairs; Gemma plays the conversation at performance 28 and he leaves satisfied and entirely certain of who she is. Outside the Hall of Submission a Black Dove steps out of a door and for a moment they can see inside: a broken body on a slab, a great ceremonial device, chanting. The bodies are turned in that hall and walk straight onto a fuelled troop transport bound for the front. Korth smells of death under its powdered perfumes, and the streets are immaculate anyway.

The listening post is an old House Sivis building, given up by the house, run for years as a members-only guild hall for accountants, and seized by the Emperor. The brass plate outside names a guild of surveyors. The door is locked; Eric presses his Mark of Passage to it and it opens without a sound. Inside is a long high room of red-cushioned lodge chairs and two long tables covered in maps weighted with carved markers, a brass frame of small glass pipes powering a handful of Sharn-style terminals that are plainly newer than everything around them, black cables running to grey cabinets, and about ten people working quietly. Messages scroll in from elsewhere and are copied into ledgers, sealed and handed to couriers who either walk out the front door or vanish where they stand. Among the papers are the remnants of body modifications and brain chips. At the dais stand a man in a director's uniform, an officer, an agent, and a creature none of them can place: the head of a hippopotamus, the bearing and uniform of a naval officer, a gun like nothing they have seen. It grunts; the agent translates into common for the director; between them sits a globe of the world. The party takes it for local colour and moves on.

Gemma sees a document on the table with FOXTALE written across the top, walks the length of the room, and slides it into her clutch with an invisible mage hand at sleight of hand 23. A clerk she tries to question tells her to do her own job. Eric reads over the map table at investigation 24 and finds a campaign map of the continent thick with fresh markers, nearly all of them struck through and dated within the last week, a heavy black X on Zilspar and a black circle drawn around the Korramont. The terminal recognises Deirdre, offers her nothing but her own case file, and gives her the station index, which will locate any file by name in a minute instead of ten. She pulls the file on the Grand Lodge.

The director comes up behind her while she is holding it. His name tag reads Chandler. Star Advocate, he says, I am not accustomed to seeing you here; usually I send you the convictions that we need and you obtain them. It is not generally a two-way conversation. He is an old man with close-cropped white hair in a dark coat buttoned to the throat, and he has entirely the wrong idea about why she has come. He read the file, he is sorry it was hers, it had to go to somebody, and he is enough of an admirer of hers to tell her in no unequivocal terms that her sister is an agent for the enemy, and to show her a little of the evidence if it will put her mind at ease. He opens a small box of ghost chips and implants, plugs a wire into one, and puts it on the display: a point-of-view recording of Bantide bent over a bloody patient, collapsing and laughing at having saved him, and a toast to success and to fallen comrades, and the hand holding the glass is Izaak's. Three or four more clandestine meetings follow. These are taken directly from the ghost chip of a known Fulcrum orchestrator, he says, one of their primary agents, and it has led to a tremendous amount of activity which you will be an important part of. Gemma plays anger at seventeen with advantage and it reads as a sister's.

Pressed on the source at deception 24, Chandler gives up the rest. We have him. He is not as useful as we hoped he would be, but his attachments are. We have been able to harvest the ghost chips from almost every Fulcrum member we have captured, we have a near complete understanding of their organisation, and it is only a matter of time until we kill every single one of them. But we are lucky to have Mr Izaak here in the listening station. Then, having said too much, he changes the subject: the opera is starting back up for one week only and he has boxed seats, and perhaps when she returns from her assignment she would care to attend the Ring Cycle with him. She cannot turn him down. She gives him a look on the way out and calls it a date. Behind him, at perception twenty, is a steel door at the back of the room with a guard on it.

Eric is caught reading confidential files by an officer who tells him the material has been sequestered from advocacy and that reading it risks tainting the advocate's own investigation and her standing with the court. They put the file down and leave.

Outside they work the problem in a small park. Dimension door reaches five hundred feet and does not require line of sight. Gemma casts it twice into the Ring of Spell Storing, takes a short rest to recover the slots, and Dario attunes to the ring, which leaves all three of them with a way in and a way out. In that hour Gemma reads her own file. FOXTALE carried no likeness until recently, when it was updated to confirm she is a tiefling. Operating out of Korth alone, which was true until a fortnight ago. Months as a novice of the Black Doves in the Korth house. Prior service with the Nest in Sharn. Traced to the rebellion at Leef and possibly to the disaster at Newham. A long section on the assassination of Cobb Cornwell. Her connection to Eric of Sharn and the whole Sharn campaign documented in full from the fall of the city. And, at the end, the record of a detention team sent to her safe house in this town shortly after she left for Sharn, which found the address empty and the subjects gone by hours. Leaving for the summit is the only reason she was not taken with Bantide and Izaak and the rest. The file's real work is resolving a dozen separate descriptions into one person. Gemma's conclusion is that the building needs to burn.

Three dimension doors put them ten feet past the steel door, into a small back room. A death trooper, a white-coated doctor and a black-coated torturer, a man on a rack who is conscious and screaming, and Izaak on a table in traction. His leg has been removed and hangs from a hook on the wall. So has a large piece of the side of his neck, where the implants were. He is under a paralytic and barely conscious. The doctor looks up, more concerned than afraid, and says she does not believe they are authorised to be in here.

Gemma feeds Izaak a healing potion as a bonus action, starts unfastening him, and then holds dimension door, waiting for him to wake and meet her eye. She holds it through a torturer's spiked weapon, through two blasts from the death trooper's gun, and through a rifle butt to the head, making concentration saves the whole way and passing the last on a natural twenty. Dario runs past the torturer to reach Izaak, eats the opportunity attack, fails the stun and freezes. Eric burns the lesser restoration that clears the paralytic, discovers a beat too late that it was a bonus action all along, and puts his dagger into the torturer's side with a true strike: Locke Pierce sends his regards. Izaak wakes screaming, Gemma's held spell fires, and she is gone with him to the apartment, telling Eric to get out and not to be brave, and to get the leg.

Doctor Ilse Brandt does not surrender. She reaches into her coat for a syringe, misses Gemma and breaks the needle, calls for an alarm that the room does not have, then puts a second syringe to the stunned Dario's throat and demands they throw down their weapons. Dario shakes off the stun on his own turn, triggers the haste crystal, ignites Pale Fire and crits, and cuts her in half in one blazing slash. The death trooper crits him twice bare-handed for twenty-one, grapples him round the head and starts working its gun up to his face. The gun goes off; the room beyond the door is coming. Eric takes the leg off the wall, puts a hand on Dario's shoulder, and Dario — who is a paladin looking at a torturer and a doctor who tortures for the state — says his flesh is willing and his spirit is weak, and lets himself be taken out. They leave the torturer alive, the man on the rack un-cut-down and unidentified, and witnesses who heard a rebel invoke Locke Pierce by name.

The city goes to alarm within minutes. Medicine at twenty-two on Izaak: every piece of cybernetics removed with no regard for his survival or for infection, open cranial wounds roughly seven days old, poisoned, one hit point, exhausted, near death, and possibly permanently brain-damaged. Dario spends lay on hands to clear the poison and the pain goes out of his face. Eric, who has two prepared slots he has never filled, takes teleportation circle, and then discovers that it consumes rare inks worth fifty gold, which nobody has and which nobody knows where to buy. Bantide screws the prosthetic back onto the bone shaft — she fitted it in the first place — and it clicks home loudly enough that Izaak cries out, and there are soldiers in the stairwell.

What follows is a farce with a bomb in it. Gemma goes out as Deirdre to buy ink and finds the nearest thing to a contact she has: the coffee vendor she once worked for, a dwarf in a cowboy hat named Juan Valdez who speaks a Colombian dialect of Dwarvish she does not know. Two coffees are mimed successfully; ink is not. At the bridge a checkpoint officer tells her the section is under lockdown and that he does not care if the Emperor told her to go and pick up doughnuts, and her persuasion misses by one. She comes back to find a squad of stormtroopers with a charge strapped to her door and a ten-second count running. She opens with what the fuck are you doing to my door, produces her identification, and stalls at performance twenty by dropping her keys and bending slowly for them, long enough for Dario to fold Izaak and Bantide into two bags of holding and for Eric to disguise himself as a long-haired high elf with cold cream on his face. The search finds nothing, and the captain apologises to the Star Advocate. On the second coffee run Juan Valdez produces the stationer's ink, and Gemma pays five hundred gold for a hundred gold's worth: two castings.

While she is out, a folder is pushed under the door and the courier is gone before Eric can look. Imperial letterhead, from Vice Admiral Freyd Merkin, two words: contact me. They decide to be already on the boat.

Eric draws the circle around Izaak because Izaak cannot be moved, and aims it at the penthouse in Sharn that was Esther Crona's. Izaak surfaces while the ink is going down. It was Pierce, he says. Pierce's intelligence. They had some kind of back door with the Empire. I think he gave me up. He learns that Zilspar is already gone and that they took his mother, and he takes it in silence. Everything is compromised, he tells them, nothing is safe, that building has to end, you have to do it, now. He is a wreck of exposed spine and moving neck muscle and a leg that looks bitten off. Gemma's parting words to him are don't eat the pomegranate. The circle lights, the far room shows through it empty, and the two of them are gone. The ink leaves a permanent stain on the floor, so Dario spends one of the four pots of Nolzur's Marvelous Pigments painting a rug over it, and the apartment is abandoned for good. They take a short rest and dimension door to the city gates. The audience, not the party, sees Freyd Merkin arrive at the door with a stormtrooper minutes later, order it opened, look around an empty room and scratch his head.

Outside the walls the war stops being a rumour. The suburbs are tent cities and garrisons, masses of starving people, no sanitation, troops moving on every road, all of it visible within a few hundred yards of a capital that keeps its streets swept. Nobody looks twice at three Imperial officers walking down to the landing. The boat is a long white-oak barge with elegant sails, flat and slow, about eighty souls, pilgrims and pensioners boarding aft for steerage and dignitaries forward under guard, with Imperial-stamped supplies for the Summer Palace going into the hold. The guard on this run wears pure white rather than red and stands as still as the rest. At the gangway an officer scans them with a wand, learns nothing about the seeming, and then reads Dario's forged weapons papers, crits her inspection, and announces that they expired three days ago. She is lying. Her advice, delivered dryly, is to start with the offer rather than the demand; a hundred gold does not move her and four hundred does.

The passage takes the night, driven upstream against the current by magic to the navigable head of the river, with the sun going down over the King's Forest to the west, where the fighting is. The upper deck has free drink, a two-piece band, standing tables and about twenty people. General Stockton Thar is aboard in head-to-toe red with a dragon staff and the old Crimson Sun regalia — the dark druid Esther once used to find the Forge of Binding, whom they first met at Posay — and he is three years older and much diminished, pressing officers for something they cannot give him. At perception fifteen they catch: we are running out of time, and if we do not give her something then I think she will be able to convince the Emperor to ring the bells. The rest is air cover for the units fighting the Uruk-hai in Ondair, and the answer to that is that it is not up to him, because the Emperor has taken personal control of the airships since Lyrandar died and wants everything protecting the Lazaret. End of discussion.

Also aboard: the Archbishop of Arawai, a Sovereign Host figure of the Dalai Lama sort whom everyone likes and whom even this Empire tolerates, personable and telling stories to a circle of nobles and Black Doves, wearing a large and obviously magical amulet. Rotunda Goose, the parliamentarian from the Thronehold summit, who survived it minus a left hand and an ear, demanding more champagne and complaining that a war veteran should not pay full fare, and hoping the doves will fix her. A very tall masked Black Dove, in white here as they all are on this run, whom Gemma recognises as the sister she saw behind the altar in the Hall of Submission that afternoon turning corpses into soldiers. Merchants, all of them bound for the Grand Lodge. Nobles with ailments, and in steerage a whole deck of the wounded coming to ask the Holy Empress to heal them, because the Lazaret is the most famous hospital in the world and is known to work miracles.

And one elf woman alone at the window, whom neither of them has ever seen, otherworldly and perfectly turned out in a gown of no common cut. Eric stands beside her and mentions his great-grandmother's tales of the sunset over Aerenal. She has never been either; she has lived her whole life in Khorvaire and has only just come here. She is going to take the vows and join the Sisterhood, which her mother calls the future of elvendom in the world. Poverty, virtue and chastity. This boat ride, she says, might be her last chance. Eric feels a great deal of pity for her and goes to eat the carrots. Her name is Saturnia.

Gemma spends her sending stone on Joseph Roberts — marriage proposal accepted — and gets back: always great to have a future ex, where are you. Later, from the lodge, she spends the night's message on Jessica: near target, cover kept, need the latest on Joseph Roberts, and the listening post in the northwest of the city needs destroying. Jessica answers that they have received Izaak, that he is well, and that they have received Nightingale too, and asks for her status. Izaak gets a codename out of it at last, since nobody had ever given him one: Ace.

They dock at midnight. The high-value passengers go off first at the good dock and the cargo and steerage go round to a smaller wooden one. Dario's divine sense says the guards on the quay are undead. A sister, unused to being spoken to, tells them that those not interested in prayer go to the Grand Lodge. A half-orc carriage driver called Pork Broccoli takes sixty gold, tells them to come and find him if they need anything while they are here, and drives them past closed market stalls, an Imperial administration building older than the Empire and hung with its banners, a reflecting pond, a triumphal gate, and out onto a gravel road into country. South, beyond rolling hills, is the northern wall of the Summer Palace itself. In the middle of the town is a hill, and on it the great hospital and holy complex that was once a prestigious Sovereign Host cathedral with a bishopric close to the Emperor, and is now the heart of the Black Doves. At the top of its great stair is a huge altar with a giant statue of Uriel, and in the courtyard, lit and suspended in the air, is a floating geometric shape.

The Grand Lodge is an old hunting lodge on the river, well appointed and nothing like the white marble of the holy buildings, with a valet who greets madames and messieurs and observes that Imperial officers are generally quartered in Imperial facilities, but that they are happy to take her money. Inside are anterooms, a room where guests deposit weapons, statuary from several eras and banners from the Kingdom of Karrnath from before there was an Empire. The only room available is the Fieldmaster's Suite at five hundred gold a night, full rate, because they are not members and the member rate is not something he is at liberty to discuss. Dario hands over fifty platinum and tells him to leave them alone. There is a king bed and a bath, a window west over the administration building and the night market and the ships coming in, and south to the Summer Palace wall and the Imperial landing pad. The crickets are loud. It is, as the Emperor evidently decided, the most beautiful climate in the world.

Gemma spends her free casting of contact other plane going to sleep, and dreams the boat again, empty except for herself and Kitsune, who is wearing the Archbishop's robes and the Archbishop's amulet and looking at the water. He makes a holy sign over her, ironically, and asks whether she has come to become a nun. She tells him she has come to retrieve his gift. You are close, he says; if you do not hurry you will have to steal it screaming. On the question of what happens to the bargain if she dies, he allows that it was never an immediate binding agreement and that it settled in somewhere around the eighth or ninth person she killed with the power he gave her. Then he explains himself, which he has never done. He has no children either. They say that when you become a father your heart is suddenly living outside your body, and he wants that — not with any child, but with the child. He intends to take it out of this world entirely, and she will not have to see it again for a very long time.

Then he corrects the terms. She has been carrying a velvet-lined lead box to bring him the heart of Caius, and she has had it wrong the whole time: the heart of the Emperor was a riddle, and what he wants is the Emperor himself. The child. Alive or dead, he does not mind. How, she asks, can you kill something that is already dead — and he tells her to think about what would need a great amount of dragon blood, and she gets there, because she has met a vampire and helped kill one. What the elf witch has dug into, he says, is some of the oldest magic there is. She has found a way to sire a child from the bones of the earth, from the seed of existence, and what she has taken out of that broken wreck of a creature is very rare, and she will protect it at all costs. Gemma tells him that everything she does is not for him, that it is for some semblance of normalcy in the world, and that handing him a child powerful enough to destroy them is handing another bomb to another ally, like Locke Pierce. He says he knows most of what she does but does not pay attention to all of it, in the same way that not everything he does is for her. She offers him a summary of her own career — that she set out to avenge her father and has since killed a great many people she judged to be bad — and he turns from the rail and asks whether she thinks they have even started yet, and tells her not to call him if what she is going to give him is this crybaby nonsense. The last thing he says before the boat rocks into fog and she is dropped into a rough night is that the time is almost near, and to get him what he wants before she rings the bell.

They end the session as paying guests inside the building they were sent to investigate, two hours south of the Ivory Lazaret, with Izaak and the Nightingale safely in Sharn, the Empire's intelligence service holding a card on a callsign it cannot put a face to, a dinner invitation to the opera from the man who runs that service, an unanswered summons from Merkin, and a patron who has just told Gemma that the thing he has been steering her toward for the entire campaign is the Emperor's unborn heir.
